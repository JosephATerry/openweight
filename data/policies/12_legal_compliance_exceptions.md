# Legal and Compliance Exception Policy

## Purpose and boundaries

This fictional policy defines how temporary exceptions to enterprise policies are requested, evaluated, approved, recorded, and closed. It does not allow any person to waive law, regulation, court order, contractual restriction, sanctions requirement, or another party's rights.

## Exception request

An exception request must identify the policy and control, business objective, affected systems and data, requested start and end dates, risk if approved, risk if denied, alternatives considered, and proposed compensating controls. The request must name a business owner and a technical owner. Missing planning time or convenience alone is not a sufficient justification.

The accountable policy owner must approve every exception. Security, Privacy, Finance, HR, Procurement, or other subject-matter approval is also required when its domain is affected. Legal and Compliance review requests involving regulated data, investigations, legal holds, cross-border transfer, contract conflict, sanctions, or government interaction. Requesters and implementers cannot approve their own exceptions.

## Duration and operation

Exceptions must be narrowly scoped and time-bound, normally no longer than 90 days. The record must specify monitoring, access limits, data handling, review frequency, and the condition that ends the exception. Multi-factor authentication, audit logging, and named-user accountability may not be removed merely to accelerate work; alternative controls require explicit Security approval.

Emergency conditions may justify verbal authorization from an authorized owner when delay would create material safety, security, legal, or service harm. The decision, scope, and compensating controls must be entered into the exception or incident system as soon as available and reviewed within one business day.

## Closure and evidence

Owners must remove temporary access, exports, vendor connections, bypasses, and configuration changes by expiration. Renewal requires a new risk review and evidence that remediation is progressing. Exception records, approvals, monitoring evidence, and closure confirmation are retained for audit. An expired exception provides no continuing authority.
